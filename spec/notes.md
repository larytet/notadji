Racing use case: in FPV racing the pilot's eyes are mostly focused in one place (the line ahead, the next gate), so a fixed or slowly moving high-resolution region (static centre-weighted, head or camera direction) captures most of the benefit without gaze-tracking latency; high speed makes it necessary to lock the view on that region. Competitive racing does not forgive loss of attention, so the design rule is that the high-resolution region must never be wrong where the pilot looks: wide region, soft edges, fast fallback to full uniform quality. Main driver: the race mode (T-05 option D, SYS-009, 25 links at 5 MHz) has a small bitrate per pilot, so concentrating bits in the middle of the image is the difference between a usable and a blurry picture; at race-track ranges bitrate, not range, is the constraint. | 10 | ANT-006/008/009/010, CAL-010, MAC-011, PHY-006, new requirement needed; links to entries 6, 7 | Angle of arrival (direction finding) from the known preamble in the ground module. One dipole alone gives no direction (a single amplitude is ambiguous); a switched-beam sector array (ANT-008) gives a coarse angle from per-beam preamble level (about 60 degrees, finer by interpolation); two or more antennas give a real angle from the phase difference of the per-branch channel estimates (phase = 2 pi d sin(theta) / lambda; at d = lambda/2 a 5 degree phase error is about 1.6 degrees of angle; two antennas give one angle, three or more non-collinear give azimuth and elevation; MUSIC/ESPRIT for several paths). Wide baselines (external antennas, entry 7) give finer angle but are ambiguous, so a coarse short-baseline estimate is needed first, and they require phase-coherent chains (shared clock, calibrated and temperature-tracked cable delay and phase), which plain MRC diversity does not. ANT-009/CAL-010 already provide the per-element calibration but only for forming beams. Combine with MAC-011 (round-trip delay from the preamble, 0.1 us, about 15 m): bearing plus range gives a position fix of the drone from the link alone, without GNSS (tracker aiming, GNSS-denied flying, cross-check of telemetry position). Limits: multipath bias (low-altitude ground reflection biases elevation), drone motion and attitude (needs a tracking filter and the antenna pattern), calibration drift looks like bearing error. Facts checked and found correct:
- Path loss at 915 MHz is 16.0 dB lower than at 5.8 GHz (20 log 5800/915).
- Quarter-wave antenna length is 8.2 cm at 915 MHz and 1.3 cm at 5.8 GHz (6.3 times longer).
- doc 02 row "915 MHz, 30 km, 3/8 dBi, MRC, 5 MHz": FSPL 121.2 dB, best MCS 16QAM 3/4, margin at BPSK 1/2 of 19.8 dB.
- Video net rate gain of split-band over in-band TDD is about 8 to 11% (20.8 against 18.7 to 19.3 Mbit/s).

- I said option B was close to the current plan. In the current source, D is the baseline and A is the fallback.
