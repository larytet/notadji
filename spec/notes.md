| 5 | 01 section 1 ("same silicon, configured by role"), ASC-001/002, ADA-001..004, RF-021, PHY-006, T-08, T-13, T-15 | Asymmetric air and ground units: in more advanced set-ups allow two different silicons (air module and ground module) instead of the same silicon in both roles. The ground module is not limited by size, mass or power the way the air unit is, so it can: (a) own and drive the frequency-hopping logic and the channel plan, the air unit follows; (b) scan the RF environment continuously (spectrum sensing, interference ranking, DFS/radar detection) with a dedicated scan receiver, without taking airtime from the video receiver; (c) use better analogue filters (preselector, narrower, lower loss) and a lower-noise front end (RF-021 target is NF 3.5-4.0 dB); (d) use better analogue-to-digital converters (higher resolution and dynamic range for blocking and adjacent-channel cases, RF-022/023/029) and more receive branches than the 2 of PHY-006; (e) spend more compute on decoding and interference handling. Keep the on-air protocol and the air-unit silicon unchanged, so a basic ground module (same silicon) still interoperates. Consequences: split the requirements by role (air-unit-only, ground-unit-only, common); the P2 two-die plan (T-08) may need an RX-heavy ground variant; the uplink is still limited by the ground transmit power and EIRP rules, so range gains come from the ground receive side and from an out-of-band (sub-GHz) upstream if used. Add a trade study (ground-unit enhanced configuration: symmetric 2-RX / enhanced RX with scan receiver and better filters and ADCs / plus tracking array). | Open |
| 6 | Details for entry 5: PHY-006, ANT-008/009, RF-021, CAL/ADA-001/007, MAC-006/007, T-01, T-13, doc 02 section 1.1a | Ground-unit levers (the ground unit can be power-hungry, BOM cost is secondary). (1) More receive branches: 4 to 8 instead of 2 (PHY-006), about +3 dB per doubling from array gain plus much better fading and shadowing resilience; the biggest single downlink lever, no regulatory change. (2) Better receive antennas: high-gain patch or helical, switched-beam array (ANT-008), tracking dish or phased array (ANT-009); T-01 shows 10 dBi gives 10 km at 5.8 GHz where 5 dBi gives 5.8 km; a 20 dBi dish with tracker only for fixed sites, not goggles. (3) Lower-noise front end: RF-021 asks NF 3.5-4.0 dB, a better LNA, preselector filters and a larger PCB can reach 1.5-2 dB (about 2 dB sensitivity). (4) Heavier decoding: more LDPC iterations, soft (chase) combining across retransmitted slices (MAC-006), longer channel-estimation window; latency limit is the 0.5 ms decode in the budget, a parallel decoder spends area to hold it. (5) Interference handling: continuous wideband scan receiver (ADA-001, ADA-007) without taking video airtime, separate DFS/radar detector, adaptive notch filters, interference cancellation with the extra antennas. (6) Uplink is the weak direction: FCC general rule cuts ground conducted power dB for dB above 6 dBi (doc 02 1.1a), so more ground power or antenna gain does not help the uplink; the 10 dBi Class X antenna gains nothing there. Fixes: lower-rate uplink MCS (MAC-007 already), out-of-band sub-GHz upstream for telemetry/RC (T-15 option C, ADA-005; about 16 dB less path loss than 5.8 GHz, but second RF chain, larger antenna, duty-cycle and region limits; keep the 1 ms ACK/CSI feedback in-band, MAC-004/005), optional 2-TX STBC/beamforming from the ground unit (PHY-006 TX is O; array gain counts toward the EIRP limit). The air unit's receive side (size, mass, power) is the real uplink bottleneck. | Open |
