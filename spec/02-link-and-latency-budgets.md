# 02 - Link, Latency and Power Budgets

Status: DRAFT, pass 2. Numbers are design targets [TBV] unless a source is named.
Link numbers are generated by `scripts/link_budget.py` (run it to reproduce).

## 1. Link budget

Model: free-space path loss `FSPL = 32.44 + 20 log10(d_km) + 20 log10(f_MHz)`, 30 dBm conducted TX power,
1 dB cable/connector loss at each end, receiver sensitivity from RF-020 scaled by `10 log10(BW/20 MHz)`.
Net rate assumes the data-tone counts of PHY-001 at 78.125 kHz spacing (48, 108, 234, 468 data tones for 5, 10, 20, 40 MHz),
a 13.6 us symbol (12.8 us + 0.8 us guard), 85% protocol efficiency, and the downlink share of the TDD frame from section 4
(uplink slot sized for MAC-007, guard from the range, frame length 1, 2 or 4 ms by range). Required margin: 6 dB (SYS-003).

| Scenario | FSPL dB | Best MCS with >= 6 dB margin | Net rate Mbit/s | Margin at BPSK 1/2 dB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.8 GHz, 10 km, 5/5 dBi, 20 MHz | 127.7 | none (fails SYS-003) | - | 3.3 |
| 5.8 GHz, 10 km, 5/5 dBi, 2-branch MRC, 20 MHz | 127.7 | BPSK 1/2 | 6.4 | 6.3 |
| 5.8 GHz, 10 km, 5/10 dBi patch, MRC, 20 MHz | 127.7 | QPSK 3/4 | 19.3 | 11.3 |
| 5.8 GHz, 10 km, 5/10 dBi patch, MRC, 10 MHz | 127.7 | QPSK 3/4 | 8.2 | 14.3 |
| 2.4 GHz, 15 km, 5/5 dBi, 20 MHz | 123.6 | BPSK 1/2 | 6.3 | 7.4 |
| 2.4 GHz, 15 km, 5/5 dBi, MRC, 10 MHz | 123.6 | QPSK 3/4 | 8.0 | 13.4 |
| 915 MHz, 30 km, 3/8 dBi, MRC, 5 MHz | 121.2 | 16QAM 3/4 | 5.8 | 19.8 |

### 1.1 Findings

1. **SYS-003 as written (5 dBi antennas, 10 km at 5.8 GHz) does not close for 1080p60 video.** With plain
   5/5 dBi antennas there is no MCS with 6 dB margin; with MRC the link closes only at BPSK 1/2
   (about 6 Mbit/s, margin 6.3 dB), which is too low for 1080p60. Either the ground antenna must be 10 dBi or better
   (patch/helical), or the bandwidth must be reduced, or the range requirement must be restated per
   antenna class. This is carried to `06` (G-01) and the options to `07` (T-01).
2. At 2.4 GHz the free-space loss is 4 dB lower than at 5.8 GHz, so a 15 km range with 5 dBi antennas is
   comparable to 10 km at 5.8 GHz.
3. Free space is optimistic. Real links need a Fresnel clearance and a ground-reflection allowance; the
   6 dB margin in SYS-003 does not include rain, foliage or body shadowing (see G-02).
4. Sensitivity follows one noise model (RF-020 rev 0.2): thermal noise in 20 MHz is -101 dBm, plus a noise
   figure of 4 dB, plus the required SNR (2 dB for BPSK 1/2, 7 dB QPSK 3/4, 15 dB 16QAM 3/4, 21 dB 64QAM 3/4 with
   LDPC), plus 2 dB implementation loss: -93, -88, -80 and -74 dBm. Pass 1 had asked for -94 dBm at BPSK 1/2, 1 dB better
   than this model, and looser values for the higher MCS; this was inconsistent (G-03, resolved in rev 0.2). The
   target is still tight and needs the short-codeword LDPC to perform near the limit.

### 1.1a Uplink (ground unit transmits, air unit receives)

The uplink carries feedback, telemetry and optional control (MAC-004, MAC-007). The air unit receives with two branches (3 dB MRC,
5 dBi antennas). The ground unit transmits at 27 dBm conducted at most; with a ground antenna above 6 dBi the FCC general rule
reduces conducted power dB for dB (30 dBm minus the excess over 6 dBi; the point-to-point exemption at 5.8 GHz applies only to
fixed installations, so it is not assumed for a mobile ground unit [rule text read in older CFR editions, to be re-checked]).
Sensitivity for QPSK 1/2 is -90 dBm (SNR 5 dB, same model as RF-020).

| Class | GU antenna dBi | GU conducted dBm (FCC rule) | Range km | Margin at QPSK 1/2 dB | Margin at BPSK 1/2 dB | Max range for QPSK 1/2 at 6 dB km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 5 | 27 | 5 | 6.3 | 9.3 | 5.2 |
| X | 10 | 26 | 10 | 4.3 | 7.3 | 8.2 |
| L | 5 | 27 | 14 | 5.0 | 8.0 | 12.5 |

Finding: the 1 Mbit/s uplink of MAC-007 (QPSK 1/2) keeps 6 dB margin only to about 5 km in Class S, 8 km in Class X and 12.5 km in
Class L. At the full range of each class only the BPSK 1/2 uplink (about 300 kbit/s) has margin. MAC-007 is restated accordingly
(rev 0.6). The 10 dBi ground antenna of Class X does not help the uplink, because the FCC rule takes its gain back as lower
transmit power.

### 1.1b Split-band uplink (trade study T-03, iteration 4)

Instead of an uplink slot inside the video band, feedback, telemetry and control use a narrowband (5 MHz) channel in another band, so the
video band is downlink-only (5% idle kept for frame-based-equipment rules and pilots). The air unit receives on one chain (no MRC gain assumed).

| Class | Video band | Uplink band | Range km | GU TX dBm | Antennas dBi (GU/AU) | Margin QPSK 1/2 at 5 MHz dB | Same-band TDD margin dB | Video net rate Mbit/s (split / TDD) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | 5.8 GHz | 2.4 GHz | 5 | 27 | 5/5 | 17.0 | 6.3 | 20.8 / 18.7 |
| X | 5.8 GHz | 2.4 GHz | 10 | 27 | 5/5 | 11.0 | 4.3 | 20.8 / 19.3 |
| L | 2.4 GHz | 0.915 GHz | 14 | 27 | 3/3 | 12.4 | 5.0 | 20.8 / 19.1 |

Findings: the uplink margin rises from 4.3 to 11.0 dB at Class X (10 km) and from 5.0 to 12.4 dB at Class L, because the lower band has 7.7 dB less
free-space loss at 2.4 GHz than at 5.8 GHz and the 5 MHz channel is 6 dB more sensitive than 20 MHz; the video band needs no guard time, no
timing advance and no uplink slot, and the net video rate rises by about 8% at 10 km. The cost is a second receive chain at the air unit, a transmit
chain at 2.4 GHz (or 900 MHz) at the ground unit and TX-to-RX isolation (RF-030). The 900 MHz uplink for 2.4 GHz video is usable where the band
allows continuous operation (US 902-928 MHz); in the EU 863-870 MHz band duty-cycle limits make it unsuitable for a 1 Mbit/s uplink, so Class L in
the EU falls back to in-band TDD.

Same-band FDD (uplink in the same 5.8 GHz band, 30 MHz from the video channel) was also evaluated and rejected [E]: the air unit would transmit
30 dBm while receiving at about -96 dBm; with transmit noise around -65 dBc/MHz-class at that offset the leakage into a 5 MHz receive channel is
about -35 dBm, so about 67 dB of isolation is needed at a 30 MHz offset, which antenna isolation (about 30 dB) plus a 125 MHz-wide band filter cannot give.

### 1.2 TX power and regulation

Conducted 30 to 33 dBm is allowed in some FCC modes only with antenna gain limits. In ETSI regions the limit is
about 20 dBm EIRP (2.4 GHz) and 14 to 25 dBm EIRP (5.8 GHz, depending on the sub-band and rule).
Regional locking (ADA-006) means the headline range applies only to FCC-type regions; ETSI regions must be
specified separately (G-04). The 5.8 GHz band edges and power limits shall be re-checked by regulatory
counsel before the numbers are frozen.

## 2. Latency budget (glass to glass)

Frame time at 60 fps is 16.67 ms. A slice is 1/8 of a frame (2.08 ms of video). All stages are cut-through
(LAT-005), so each stage adds its pipeline delay, not a frame.

| Stage | 1080p60 budget ms | 1080p120 budget ms | Source requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure (mid-exposure to end, average) | 2.0 | 1.0 | sensor setting |
| Sensor readout of one slice | 1.0 | 0.5 | sensor (about 8 ms rolling-shutter readout) |
| MIPI receive and ISP-lite, one slice | 1.0 | 0.5 | IF-002 |
| Encoder (first pixel in, first bits out) | 2.0 | 1.2 | VID-007 |
| Framing, FEC encode, interleave | 0.3 | 0.2 | PHY-005 |
| TX modem and RF (including PA ramp) | 0.2 | 0.2 | RF-012 |
| Air time of one slice | 2.0 | 1.0 | LAT-003 |
| RX front end, demodulation, FEC decode | 0.5 | 0.4 | PHY-010 |
| Decoder (first bits in, first line out) | 2.0 | 1.2 | VID-008 |
| Display: line-sync scan-out, panel response, de-jitter | 4.0 | 2.5 | VID-015 |
| **Sum** | **15.0** | **8.7** | |
| Reserve (jitter, retransmission, thermal) | 3.0 | 2.0 | LAT-002 |
| **Total** | **18.0** | **10.7** | SYS-001: 20 and 12 |

Observations:

- VID-007/VID-008 are specified only for 1080p60; the 1080p120 budget assumes 1.2 ms each (G-05).
- "Air time of one slice" depends on the video bit rate and the PHY rate: at 20 Mbit/s video on a 24 Mbit/s
  PHY it is about 1.7 ms. At low MCS (the 6.8 Mbit/s case in section 1) a slice of a 6 Mbit/s stream takes
  about 2 ms, but the video bit rate must be lowered to fit, so quality, not latency, suffers first.
- The G2G latency also depends on the display. A 60 Hz panel that scans out over 16.7 ms adds up to one
  frame if the scan-out is not synchronised to the arrival of the slices (VID-015). A 120 Hz or OLED display
  with a programmable scan start is assumed.

## 3. Power budget (air unit, 1080p60, 30 dBm average conducted output, [TBV])

PA DC power = RF power / PAE. PWR-004 asks for 30% PAE with DPD. Regulator and conversion losses assume 88%
efficiency. SYS-005 excludes the camera sensor module.

| Block | ASIC target W | FPGA prototype W |
|---|---|---|
| RF PA at 30 dBm (1.0 W RF) at 30% PAE | 3.3 | 3.3 |
| Transceiver (2 TX/2 RX/obs) | 0.5 | 1.5 (AD9361-class) |
| Baseband, FEC, encoder | 0.7 | 6.5 (Zynq UltraScale+ EV class; with memory below it equals the 7.5 W typical figure of the K26 SOM) |
| Memory (LPDDR4) | 0.3 | 1.0 |
| Subtotal (excluding sensor) | 4.8 | 12.3 |
| **Total at the input, excluding sensor (/0.88)** | **5.5** | **14.0** |
| Sensor and ISP (0.8 W, through the same regulators) | +0.9 | +0.9 |
| Total including sensor | 6.4 | 14.9 |

Sensitivity of the result:

| Case | PA DC W | Input power excluding sensor W |
|---|---|---|
| 25 dBm at 30% PAE | 1.05 | 2.9 |
| 27 dBm at 30% PAE | 1.67 | 3.6 |
| 30 dBm at 30% PAE | 3.33 | 5.5 |
| 30 dBm at 25% PAE | 4.00 | 6.3 (exceeds the 6.0 W of SYS-005) |
| 33 dBm at 30% PAE | 6.65 | 9.3 (exceeds 6.0 W) |

Conclusions: SYS-005 (6.0 W) holds at 30 dBm only if the PA reaches about 27% PAE or better at the 30 dBm average
OFDM operating point; the 30% of PWR-004 is specified at 24 dBm, a different point, and typical class-AB PAs
with enough back-off for 64QAM reach less (G-06). It cannot hold at 33 dBm. See T-02 in `07`. Public DJI figures are
not available to us; the 5 to 9 W range quoted earlier in the project discussion is our estimate only.

## 4. In-band TDD frame budget (fallback mode, MAC-001)

Rules (MAC-001, MAC-007, MAC-011): the downlink to uplink guard is `2 x 3.33 us per km x range + 2 x 10 us` (round-trip delay plus two
turn-arounds); the uplink slot is sized to carry 1 Mbit/s at QPSK 1/2 (234 data tones per symbol at 20 MHz) plus 100 feedback bits per
frame plus a 2-symbol reference preamble (the burst is frame-synchronous, so no blind detection is needed; PHY-010's 40 us applies only
to the first acquisition); the frame is 1 ms up to 7 km, 2 ms up to 15 km and 4 ms beyond (T-03).

| Range km | Frame ms | UL symbols (QPSK 1/2, 1 Mbit/s + 2 preamble) | UL us | Guard us | DL share of frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1 | 7 | 95 | 53 | 0.852 |
| 7 | 1 | 7 | 95 | 67 | 0.838 |
| 10 | 2 | 11 | 150 | 87 | 0.882 |
| 15 | 2 | 11 | 150 | 120 | 0.865 |
| 30 | 4 | 20 | 272 | 220 | 0.877 |

At short range the split is about 85% downlink, 9.5% uplink and 5% guard (the "9:1" of the first draft); at 15 km the guard alone is 120 us.
An earlier draft used an 80 us uplink slot; with a 2-symbol preamble that slot carries only about 0.9 Mbit/s at QPSK 1/2, so MAC-007 could
not be met (found in the verification pass, `12`).
